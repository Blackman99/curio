What is this?

The magnets. Paint chips and their names from the xkcd colour survey (Randall Munroe, 2010; more than five million colour names typed in 222,500 sessions, summarised as its most common names, each with a representative RGB; public domain, CC0). The file has 949 names; 28 crude ones are left out, so there are 921 pairs. A chip encoder sees only the RGB: three numbers through a 32→32 ReLU network to a point on the sphere. A name encoder sees only the words: each of the 386 words is a learned arrow, and a name points along the sum of its words' arrows. The globe is the embedding space itself: three dimensions, nothing projected.

The loss. CLIP's (Radford et al. 2021) symmetric InfoNCE (van den Oord et al. 2018): in each batch of 128 pairs, every chip's similarity to every name is a cosine divided by the temperature τ, and a softmax over the names should pick its own; the same for every name over the chips. Its gradient on a chip is a pull toward its own name and a push away from each other name in the batch, weighted by that softmax. Adam, learning rate 0.01, 3,000 steps, all in float64 in your browser.

Temperature. Hot, the softmax is flat and every rival pushes equally (a median of 127.6 effective rivals out of the batch's 128 names at τ = 10), so the push is just the crowd's average, which is zero once the crowd is centred: nothing spreads the magnets evenly. Cold, it is almost a max: 2.0 effective rivals at τ = 0.002, so only near neighbours separate and the crowd spreads slowly from where it began. The widest bare patch (the largest empty cap, measured against 2,000 directions) over 30 seeds: median 121° (115–128°) at τ = 0.002 (median 91° (75–97°) after 12,000 steps), median 13° (10–15°) at 0.07, median 48° (35–57°) at 10 (median 54° (41–58°) after 12,000 steps). In all 30 seeds the τ = 0.07 globe had a smaller bare patch than both extremes. This is the alignment-and-uniformity picture of Wang & Isola (2020) and the hardness-aware temperature of Wang & Liu (2021). CLIP learns τ, starting at 0.07, clipped at 0.01; here you set it.

Never seen. 138 names (15%) are held out of training, chosen at random among names whose every word also occurs in a training name. Ranking all 921 names by closeness to a held-out chip, its own name comes 18th (median over 30 seeds at τ = 0.07; in the top ten 35% of the time); by chance it would be about 461st. Trained chips find their own name first 30% of the time and sit 4.8° from it. With only three numbers, plenty of phrases land badly: at seed 7, ‘pastel navy’ lands among apple greens.

Words. Over 30 seeds the arrows for ‘dark’ and ‘light’ end up median 148° (79–171°) apart, ‘light’ and ‘pale’ median 14° (2–36°), ‘dark’ and ‘deep’ median 21° (6–88°). The five chips nearest the ‘dark’ arrow average a CIE lightness of 33, those nearest ‘light’ 74 (0–100 scale, medians over seeds).

What is real. The data, the loss, every gradient and every reading are computed, not animated. The engine uses its own exp and ln series and a fixed summation order, and an independent numpy implementation reproduces it bit for bit: parameters, Adam moments and every loss over 10 runs of up to 6,000 steps, plus all 1,842 positions; a finite-difference check agrees with the analytic gradient to 6×10−5. The simplifications: CLIP embeds images and captions with large networks into 512 or more dimensions and uses batches of 32,768; here the “images” are single colours, the batch is 128, word order is ignored (‘blue green’ = ‘green blue’) and the embedding has three dimensions so you can see all of it.
