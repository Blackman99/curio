What is this?

The formula. Take any finite set of target points xi. Flow matching’s straight-line paths go xt = (1−t)x0 + t·xi, with x0 Gaussian noise and each xi equally likely. The velocity a network is trained to regress, the average of (xi − x0) over every wire through a point, has a closed form here: ut(x) = (Σ wixi − x)/(1−t), with weights wi ∝ exp(−‖x − t·xi‖²/2(1−t)²). This page integrates that with Euler steps in float64 and draws each path (Lipman et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2023; Albergo & Vanden-Eijnden, 2023).

Smoke and wires. The wires are the training targets: each noise sample tied to a random target. The smoke is the flow of their average. The theorem is that the two have the same distribution at every t, which the laser sheet lets you check: sliced W₁ is the mean 1-D Wasserstein distance over 8 directions, printed beside the distance between two independent draws of wires, which is what sampling noise alone gives you. With few Euler steps the smoke drifts away from the wires, and that number grows.

How typical is this? Over 10 seeds on the airliner (2,400 threads, 640 targets, 64 steps), wires travel 10.6–11.9× the smoke’s squared distance, and at t = ¼, ½ and ¾ the smoke-to-wires distance is below the wires’ own noise floor in 20 of 30 cuts, never above 1.46× it. Rectified flow’s theorem says the flow can’t cost more than its wires for any convex cost. Three posts split the rake at the Gaussian third-quantiles, ±0.431 (a fine RK4 solve of 6,001 nozzles gives 2000/2001/2000). Eight knots with 100,000 threads share out evenly (χ² = 5.7 on 7 degrees of freedom).

The steps. Threads within 0.01 of a target on the default airliner: 0% with 1 Euler step, 3.5% with 4, 5.1% with 16, 25.6% with 32, 81.7% with 64, 99.1% with 128. The field is stiff near t = 1, so Euler needs many small steps there; at 64 steps the ends sit a mean 0.03 from a 1,024-step RK4 solve, and 68% pick the same target (the rest land on a neighbouring point).

The memoriser. Near t = 1 one target’s weight goes to 1, so the exact field can only land on the training points. A trained network never fits this field exactly, and Bertrand et al. (2025) argue that this failure, mostly early in the flow, is where generalisation comes from. No network is trained on this page.

Real and not. The noise, the wires, the weights, the Euler steps and every number are computed in your browser and match a separate numpy implementation bit for bit. The target shapes are hand-drawn polygons, stippled by best-candidate sampling. The tunnel is a metaphor: this is not a fluid simulation, the horizontal axis is time, and there is no pressure, viscosity or turbulence. In the ¾ and axial views threads can appear to cross only because a 3-D curve is being projected onto your screen; the flat three-post rake in the side window shows the real rule. The beads are tracers along the same paths, moving at a constant pace for show. The look borrows from F. N. M. Brown’s smoke tunnel photographs at Notre Dame.
