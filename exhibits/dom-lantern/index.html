What is this?

Paper Lantern is a 3D pleated paper lantern folded entirely out of ordinary HTML elements and CSS 3D transforms — zero <canvas>, zero WebGL, and zero SVG inside the lantern. The lantern holds 160 trapezoidal paper facets arranged across 10 pleated rings and 16 vertical gores, plus 32 collar panels, scalloped trim, an inner paper candle, a hanging cord, and a paper tassel — 2,437 live DOM nodes in all.

Why only real DOM + CSS 3D can do this

Every colored paper strip is still a real paragraph of HTML text inside <article id="lantern">. While it is folded into 3D you can highlight words across curved pleats (switch to Select text), run browser find-in-page (Ctrl/⌘F or the Find button), translate the page with your browser's built-in translator, inspect any facet in DevTools, or read the full essay through a screen reader.

Clicking Unfold to article does not swap in a hidden copy: the back seam opens along a continuous coaxial unrolling curve, and at 0% the exact same <article>, <section>, and <span> nodes drop their CSS transforms and flow as a normal scrollable web page. Clicking Flat CSS switches transform-style from preserve-3d to flat, collapsing the 3D volume into a flat stack of cards on the spot.

What is computed for real

  • Watertight pleated geometry: Each ring alternates in and out by 10.5 px around an oblate profile (max pleat tilt 65.5°). Every facet's top and bottom chord widths (32–72 px), slant height, trapezoid clip-path, and 4×4 CSS transform T(cx,cy,cz)·Ry(φ)·Rx(α) are solved in closed form. Across all fold states u ∈ [0, 1], adjacent facets meet edge-to-edge with max vertex gap < 10−12 px (verified against an independent Python reference implementation and browser getComputedStyle().transform readback).
  • Surface area & volume: Closed pleated paper area is 345.7k px² (113.1% of the flat rectangular spread, 305.6k px²) enclosing 19.51M px³ by the divergence theorem.
  • Paper shading: Each facet blends overhead Lambertian daylight with inverse-square inner candle transmission weighted by paper thickness (thin cream rice paper transmits 88–90%; dyed crayon-blue cardstock transmits 46–48%). Because both terms are axially symmetric, spinning the lantern only updates one parent transform per frame so the browser compositor stays at 60 fps.

What is artistic / simulated

The lantern's gentle pendulum sway on its hanging cord, the craft-table dot pattern, and the dusk-room toggle are visual touches rather than a physics or radiative-transfer simulation. The essay itself (270 words across ten rings) is an original short piece written for this exhibit.

On browsers without preserve-3d support (or when opened with ?no3d), the exhibit degrades cleanly to the unfolded flat paper article with zero errors. Note on CSS 3D performance: clipping more than ~160 separate 3D facets with clip-path: polygon() slows down software rasterizers, so only the 160 body trapezoids are polygon-clipped while the collars, candle, cord, and tassel use unclipped planes.
