What is this?

Type a formula and it is taken apart into one tank per operation, in the order a computer would do the arithmetic: a tape, or computational graph. The reservoirs at the top are its inputs.

Forward pass: water runs down; each tank's level is its value, on a logarithmic gauge with 0 at the brass mid-line (marks at ±1, ±10, ±100).

Backward pass: dye goes in at the bottom, f̄ = 1, and is pumped back up. Each pipe multiplies the dye it carries by its local derivative (through sin, by cos of its input; through ×, by the other factor). Where a value fed several pipes, their dye adds up. The colour that collects in a tank is ∂f/∂ that tank: red positive, blue negative, stronger for larger. That is reverse-mode automatic differentiation, which is what backpropagation is.

All of it is real arithmetic in your browser, in double precision. The check line under the gradient nudges each input by a millionth either way and compares. Open the valve runs gradient descent: every input moves against its own dye, x ← x − η·x̄.

The default formula is the worked example in Baydin, Pearlmutter, Radul & Siskind, Automatic Differentiation in Machine Learning: a Survey (JMLR, 2018), Table 3. The board is after Bill Phillips's MONIAC (1949), which computed the British economy with water. Ours only pretends to be wet.

Functions: sin cos tan exp ln sqrt tanh sigmoid relu abs, with + − * / ^, brackets, pi and up to four one-letter inputs. AD differentiates the arithmetic as written, so sqrt(x − x) reads NaN where the plain function is flat.
