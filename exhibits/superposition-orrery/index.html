What is this?

Each planet is one input feature of a toy model. n features are squeezed into m hidden numbers, h = W x, and read back out with x̂ = ReLU(Wᵀh + b). A feature's brass arm is its column of W.

Training runs live in your browser: Adam on importance-weighted squared error, inputs uniform in [0, 1] and set to zero with probability = sparsity. Nothing is pre-baked, so every shake can land a little differently.

When features are rare, they seldom fire together. The network then stores more of them than it has dimensions and tolerates a little interference, which the negative bias and the ReLU mostly erase. That is superposition. The ruler shows each feature's share of a dimension, ‖Wᵢ‖² / Σⱼ(Ŵᵢ·Wⱼ)²; it locks onto fractions like 1/2 and 2/5 that name the shape.

After Elhage et al., Toy Models of Superposition (Anthropic, 2022). A toy, not a real language model.
