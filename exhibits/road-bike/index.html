What is this? — Road Bike Geometry & Dynamics

The Steering Paradox

Every bicycle's front fork has two numbers that shape how it feels: the head-tube angle (α) and the fork offset / rake (d). Together with wheel radius R they set the ground trail:

c = (R cos α − d) / sin α = R tan λ − d / cos λ

where λ = 90° − α is the steer-axis tilt. Two counterintuitive facts fall straight out of this equation: increasing fork offset pushes the axle forward and shortens trail; and a slacker head angle with more trail is not always more stable.

At 5.6 m/s (20.2 km/h), the steep Criterium (trail 54.7 mm, self-stable 5.18–9.46 m/s) and Endurance (62.8 mm, 5.50–10.18 m/s) already self-stabilize after a nudge, while the slack, high-trail Gravel bike (73.8 mm, 5.91–11.04 m/s) is still weave-unstable. Above 10.2 m/s (37 km/h) the roles flip: Criterium and Endurance cross capsize while Gravel remains self-stable up to 39.7 km/h.

Whipple–Carvallo Benchmark (Meijaard et al. 2007)

The stability curves and live nudge response solve the full 25-parameter linearized rigid-body equations of Meijaard, Papadopoulos, Ruina & Schwab (2007), Proc. R. Soc. A 463, 1955–1982 (Appendix A, eqs A1–A27):

M q̈ + v C₁ q̇ + (g K₀ + v² K₂) q = 0,   q = [φ, δ]ᵀ

Toggle Meijaard '07 to inspect the exact published benchmark bicycle: weave speed 4.292 m/s, capsize speed 6.024 m/s, and weave eigenvalue at 5 m/s of -0.775 ± 4.465i s⁻¹ (verified to 12+ digits against Table 2 of the paper). Even when fork offset is pushed to 105 mm on the Endurance frame—giving negative trail of -0.3 mm—the forward handlebar mass still yields a narrow self-stable window (2.79–3.01 m/s), echoing Kooijman et al. (2011).

11-Speed Drivetrain & Slant Parallelogram

Each bike carries a Shimano 11-speed road compact layout: 50/34T chainrings (7.2 mm spacing, 43.5 mm nominal chainline) and an 11–34T 11-cog cassette (34-28-24-21-19-17-15-14-13-12-11T at 3.74 mm pitch; ratios 1.00–4.55, development 2.13–9.68 m/rev).

The rear derailleur is a rigid 47° slant-parallelogram four-bar linkage (42 mm bars): shifting laterally toward larger inner cogs automatically drops the guide pulley along the cassette cone. At every shift, the page solves numerically for the tension-cage angle ψ (spanning -91.3° on 34×11 to +36.2° on 50×34, a 127.5° sweep) that keeps total roller-chain length strictly constant (112 / 114 / 116 half-inch links). Extreme cross-chaining reaches +3.09° (50×34) and -3.10° (34×11).

What Is Computed vs. Modeled

  • Computed live (float64): all frame/fork hardpoints, ground & mechanical trail, wheel flop, 25-parameter Whipple matrices (M, C₁, K₀, K₂), quartic eigenvalues (Aberth–Ehrlich), weave/capsize speeds (Routh–Hurwitz & closed-form det K), RK4 impulse response, gear ratios, chainline angle, 3D 4-bar parallelogram pins, constant-chain-length cage angle, and 3D roller-chain path.
  • Modeled / chosen: the three 700c road frames (Criterium / Endurance / Gravel) use representative size-56 geometry and a uniform 78 kg rider+frame mass model rather than a specific commercial bicycle; tube cross-sections, rim depth profiles, and studio finishes are procedural visual design. Tire slip, frame flex, and rider feedback are omitted, as in the rigid Whipple benchmark.
