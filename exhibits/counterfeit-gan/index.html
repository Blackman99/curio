What is this?

The game. Generative adversarial nets were introduced with this picture: “The generative model can be thought of as analogous to a team of counterfeiters, trying to produce fake currency and use it without detection, while the discriminative model is analogous to the police, trying to detect the counterfeit currency” (Goodfellow et al., 2014). Here both are small neural nets, trained against each other in your browser as you watch.

The chart. Every point is an ink: hue round the circle, strength outwards. The mint prints eight denominations on a ring of radius 2, each in its own ink, and genuine notes vary around their ink with standard deviation σ = 0.05. This is the ring-of-Gaussians test of Metz et al. (2016), with σ 0.05 instead of 0.02. The grid mint has 25 inks one unit apart.

The rivals. The forger turns two random numbers (each the sum of 12 uniform draws minus 6) into an ink. It is a 2→48→48→2 ReLU net with 2,594 weights. The detective turns an ink into a verdict: 2→48→48→1, leaky ReLU 0.2, 2,545 weights, or 2,641 when it checks pairs. Each round the detective takes one Adam step on 128 genuine notes and 128 forgeries (cross-entropy). The forger then takes 1, 2 or 5 Adam steps to make its notes pass, using the usual non-saturating loss −log D. Adam runs at learning rate 0.001, β₁ 0.5, β₂ 0.999. The lamp shows the detective’s verdict on a 64×64 grid; a pairs detective is shown judging two identical notes of that ink. The dashed line is 50:50.

What counts. A forgery is passable within 3σ (0.15) of a real ink, the rule used by VEEGAN and PacGAN. A denomination counts as forged once at least 1% of the 512 printed notes are passable copies of it. Variety is the notes’ spread, the RMS distance from their mean along each axis; genuine notes have 1.42. The biggest pile counts the notes within 0.15 of one ink, which is how much a genuine stack varies.

Mode collapse, measured. I ran 30 pairs of rivals (seeds 1–30) for 3,000 rounds each. With one lesson each, 29 of 30 forgers print all eight denominations by round 2,000. With five forger lessons per round, 27 of 30 collapse: their median variety after round 1,000 is 0.12, and none has all eight by round 2,000. With two lessons per round, 15 of 30 do. The GAN tutorial (Goodfellow, 2016) explains the failure: when the forger’s training sits in the inner loop, it is asked “to map every z value to the single x coordinate that the discriminator believes is most likely to be real”. In the tour that note is not even a real denomination. A forger that has already learned all eight usually stays put when you switch to five lessons; the collapse happens while it is still learning.

Pairs. PacGAN (Lin, Khetan, Fanti and Oh, 2018) changes only the detective’s input: it judges several samples at once. Genuine pairs are almost never identical, so a collapsed forger is caught at a glance. At five lessons to one with a pairs detective, 0 of 30 collapse and 25 print all eight by round 2,000. I also started from collapsed forgers (1,500 rounds at five to one) and hired a new detective. A pairs detective brought back all eight in 30 of 30 runs, with a median of 760 rounds and the forger still on five lessons. A new single-note detective at one to one managed 13 of 30 within 2,000 rounds. In PacGAN’s own 2D-ring test (radius 1, σ 0.01, much bigger nets, 400 epochs), a GAN found 6.3 of 8 modes on average and PacGAN2 found 7.9.

The tour uses seed 7. All eight denominations are forged by round 1,170. At round 2,000, 74% of forgeries are passable, and the detective passes 52% of real notes and 45% of fakes. At five lessons to one, round 663 has 501 of 512 forgeries in one pile (variety 0.06); at round 1,000 the pile holds 73%. After a pairs detective takes over at round 1,000, all eight return by round 1,750, and at round 2,500 78% are passable.

Real or simulated? All of it is really trained, in float64, as you watch: a few million multiply-adds per round. To make runs reproducible the engine never calls Math.exp; the sigmoid uses a fixed 22-term series, and every sum is added in a fixed order. A numpy reimplementation replays the page’s sessions, including rule changes and new denominations, bit for bit: every weight and Adam moment, compared by hash. Finite differences agree with the backpropagation to a relative error of 6×10−6 or better. The simplifications: real GANs make images, not points on a two-dimensional chart; a “lesson” is one gradient step; and the ink chart is a metaphor for a 2D sample space.
