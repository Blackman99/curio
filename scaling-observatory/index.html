What is this?

The sky is model space on log scales: parameters N across, training tokens D up. The glow and its contour lines are the loss (nats per token on DeepMind’s MassiveText) predicted by the parametric law of Hoffmann et al., 2022 (the Chinchilla paper, Approach 3): L(N, D) = E + A/N^α + B/D^β.

Training costs about C ≈ 6·N·D FLOPs, so one budget is one diagonal: the slit. The telescope runs a golden-section search along it for the lowest loss. On a straight slit this is the closed form N* = G·(C/6)^(β/(α+β)), which the reference implementation checks it against. The paper counts Gopher’s budget more carefully (5.76×10²³ FLOPs); 6·N·D gives 5.04×10²³.

The dots are the paper’s training runs, read back off its Figure 4 (colour = loss) by Besiroglu, Erdil, Barnett & You, 2024 (Epoch AI), rounded here to 5 significant figures; the colour scale limits each loss to about ±0.01. The five with the highest loss, large models given very few tokens, are left out unless you add them back, as Epoch did.

Lenses. Printed is the law as printed: E 1.69, A 406.4, B 410.7, α 0.34, β 0.28. It sits above 235 of the 240 runs. Unrounded uses the digits left in comments in the paper’s TeX source (α 0.33917, β 0.28491); it sits above 84 of 240. Re-ground is fitted in your browser: the same Huber loss (δ = 10⁻³ on log L, log-sum-exp form) minimised with BFGS, starting from the printed lens. On the 240 runs it takes 46 steps to E 1.8171, A 477.5, B 2141, α 0.3473, β 0.3671. Epoch reported E 1.8172, A 482.01, B 2085.43, α 0.3478, β 0.3658; this page’s fit has a slightly lower Huber sum on the same data. Per Epoch, the original fit stopped early because the loss was averaged rather than summed. Grinding from only the smaller runs shows how far the answer can move when you extrapolate.

Stars. The five models of the paper’s Table 1 and Chinchilla itself (Jurassic-1, 178B parameters on 300B tokens, sits on top of GPT-3), plus Llama 3 8B, which Meta trained on over 15T tokens (2024). Their losses here are what Chinchilla’s law predicts for that size and token count. They are not the models’ measured losses, which were taken on different data.

Serving. Following Sardana, Portes, Doubov & Frankle, 2024, each token a model serves costs about 2·N FLOPs, so the slit becomes 6·N·D + 2·N·Dinf = C and bends. They minimise the total bill for a fixed loss; the slit asks the dual question (the lowest loss for a fixed bill), which picks out the same models. With 10¹⁴ tokens served, Llama 3 8B costs 2.5% more than the best model for its loss.

Everything you see is computed live: the contours, the search, the fits, the compute-equivalent savings. A Python reference implementation reproduces every focus and every fit bit for bit. The data are a reconstruction and the FLOP count is the 6·N·D approximation.
