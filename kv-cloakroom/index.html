What is this?

The model is real. A two-layer, four-head transformer with 121,216 parameters (64-wide, learned positions for 256 letters, ReLU, weights stored as 8-bit integers) trained on four of Oscar Wilde’s plays from Project Gutenberg, one letter at a time. It runs here in plain JavaScript, in 64-bit floats. Its plays don’t make much sense; every letter of them is computed.

The cloakroom is the KV cache. In each attention layer a letter is turned into a query, a key and a value (Vaswani et al., 2017). A letter’s keys and values never change once it is written, because it can only look backwards. So the model keeps them (Pope et al., 2022), and each new letter costs one pass through the model plus one look along the racks.

The tailor has no cache. For every new letter he runs the whole text through the model again, layer by layer, with a separate piece of code. Each time he finishes, the page compares his 74 next-letter scores with the cloakroom’s: they agree to the last bit, every time. The counts are the multiply-adds both of them actually perform in the matrix products and attention (layer norms and the softmax aren’t counted). The clock is made up: both get the same number of multiply-adds per second.

Hooks are memory. Here a letter’s coat and hat are 256 numbers. In Llama 3 8B they are 65,536 (32 layers × 8 key-value heads × 128 × 2), 128 KiB at 16 bits. With a short rack, the oldest coat goes to lost property when a new guest arrives: a sliding window, as in Longformer and Mistral 7B. The page scores each rack on 2,048 letters of An Ideal Husband that the model never trained on (bits per letter, average over all 2,048). Big models also need their first few coats kept, the “attention sinks” of Xiao et al., 2023; this tiny one barely does: keeping the first four helps a rack of 32 by 0.0004 nats, and a rack of 4 by 0.013 (0.7%).

The full house is simulated. The allocator is real and runs here; the other parties are drawn at random (opening lines of 4–47 letters, up to 256 in all). Each tick every seated party writes one letter. Reserve holds 256 hooks for every party, as servers did before PagedAttention (Kwon et al., 2023, who measured only 20–38% of such memory in use). Paged hands out racks of 16 as they fill and keeps one rack spare; if none is left, the latest party to be seated gives its racks back and starts again.

A Python reference implementation reproduces every letter, every score, every multiply-add count and both house runs bit for bit.
